# Calibration notebook

The supplied DOS executable remains the reference for behavior not fixed by
the manual. This browser remake has intentionally chosen transparent, seeded
rules rather than attempting to reproduce its machine-level random sequence.
Where the manual and the executable disagree, the executable's own string table
wins; the proclamations and report columns quoted below were recovered from a
dump of that table in `ARGONAUT.COM`.

| Scenario | DOS reference behavior from manual | Web scenario | Current implementation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fleet roster | A battle cruiser, three cruisers, and scout per fleet; Xanadu participates | Any seed | 21 ships: five ships for each alliance plus Xanadu |
| Starbase | Xanadu is heavily armored and armed but unable to move; it cannot hyperspace | Take command of Xanadu and order a move or a jump | 0 engine units, so `2` and `-` are both refused and no autopilot will navigate it; its self-destruct blast is doubled instead. It is also far too massive for any tractor beam to tow, so neither the player's `5` nor an enemy beam can move it or hold it in a lock (`isImmovable`) |
| Ship names | The manual names every ship; the binary's glyph table confirms the roster | Any seed | Canonical names assigned by class and initial (Argo=A, Pequod=P, Xanadu=X, wreck=+) |
| Hull complements | The manual pins down the roster and the damage band, but states no shield or crew figure per class | Watch how long a fleet engagement runs | Shields and crew are **double** the values the weapon table was tuned against — battle cruiser 200/200, cruiser 140/140, scout 90/70, Xanadu 320/320 — so a volley takes half the bite it used to. Measured over 250 seeds: a classic war runs 24.7 stardates against 15.8 before the change, an extended war 28.3 against 16.7, and volleys per kill roughly double (14.2 from 7.8, 19.5 from 9.7). Subsystem complements are deliberately unchanged, so weapon output and sensor reach per hull stay exactly as calibrated. `SHRAPNEL_DAMAGE` doubled with them: the self-destruct blast destroys outright whatever it covers and so needed no change, but shrapnel is a flat number and would otherwise have become decorative against the new pools. Scaling the hulls rather than weakening the guns keeps the manual's damage figures intact, and keeps the proportional mechanics — dockyard recovery, hyperspace shield loss, alert thresholds, `CRIPPLE.fraction` — scaled with them. Weakening the guns instead was measured and rejected: with damage at a third, 13 of 60 extended wars never terminated at all, because the flat `SHIELD_PER_ENGINE` flush and the dockyard out-repaired everything the enemy could fire |
| Hidden reports | Roll call (R), shot distribution (S), alliance statistics (L), full war-zone map (Bksp) | Press each key | All four implemented; statistics include shots for/against, credited kills, and chances of victory. The binary's roll call is a `Ship / Alliance / Location / Distance / Status / Course` table whose Status column carries only `Active`, `Vacant`, or `Dead` — it has no cause-of-elimination code, so the manual's "code indicating if and how a ship has been eliminated" is that three-state column. The remake prints Location and Distance beside it; Course is not tracked |
| Computer report | `0` is a standard command; the manual reserves the extra information for the hidden ones, which "your enemies have no access to" | Press `0` with enemies beyond mapper range | Reports only what the mapper can see: the alliance counts, nearest enemy, and nearest ally are all limited to mapper range, so a standard command no longer out-sees `7`. The bearing to Xanadu stays unconditional, matching the binary's "Distance to Federation base Xanadu is N units." It previously named the nearest enemy and its exact range anywhere in the war zone |
| Controls | Single keystrokes, mostly along the top row; `Tab` does nothing and passes your turn | Click the map; press `Tab`, `P`, `-`, `=`, `Esc` | `Tab` still passes, but only when no control has focus — hijacking it unconditionally made the twenty-button command panel unreachable by keyboard, so `P` passes from anywhere and Shift+Tab always traverses. Clicking empty map space issues an engine order toward that point, clamped to engine capacity, through the same `moveAction` as `2` so collisions still resolve. `-`, `=`, and `Esc` confirm first, since none can be undone. A dialog's Cancel always dismisses it: the coordinate prompt's used to submit its form, and its two required fields then failed validation and trapped the player inside a dialog they could not leave. The whole page is no longer one `aria-live` region, which announced the entire board on every keystroke; one concise status region replaces it |
| Victory wording | Four proclamations, all recovered from the binary's string table: "The Federation has triumphed.", "<Alliance> forces have won.", "The war has destroyed all four alliances.  No one wins.", and "The war has ended in a hopeless draw.  All survivors are stranded." | End the war each way | All four implemented. The hopeless draw is distinct from annihilation and fires when both sides still have active hulls but nothing can ever change: either no survivor can act — every one is out of engines (so it can neither move nor hyperspace), no enemy sits inside its weapons', tractor's, or self-destruct reach, no vacant hull sits inside transporter range, and no hull is merely holding station on orders — or the whole war zone has gone unchanged for `STALEMATE_ROUNDS` (12) consecutive stardates. The stranded test catches provable cases at once; the stalemate count is the general net, and both apply in a classic war too, since hanging forever is not a calibrated behavior. The manual gives no trigger, so both are reconstructions. Every alliance is at war with every other, so the war ends only when the field is down to one alliance — or none — and the alliance proclamation names that last one standing. Losing the Federation therefore does not end a war that still has two or more alliances afloat: they fight it out among themselves, and the player, with no hull left, watches the remaining rounds play out as a spectator |
| Engine limit | A ship moves no more total units than available engines | Use `2`, enter a displacement | Capacity is 10 × working engine units; vector distance is enforced |
| Weapon range | Phasers 30; photons 10; tractor 35 | Select `3`, `4`, or `5`, then a target | Euclidean range is enforced before the shot resolves. All three limits live in one shared `RANGES` table read by the player commands, the autopilots, and the map overlay |
| Shield flush | Reinforcing shields flushes engine power | Use `1` | Requires working engines; gains `SHIELD_PER_ENGINE` (5) shields per engine unit ("Engines flushed for N units"). Left flat when the hull complements doubled, so a flush now recovers half the fraction of a pool it used to. That is deliberate: recovery outpacing the guns is exactly what made the weakened-gun configurations fail to terminate |
| Weapon accuracy | Shots can miss ("Missed!", "just missed") | Fire repeatedly; then watch an enemy volley | One shared seeded 12% `MISS_CHANCE` governs the player's volleys and the autopilots' alike; a miss still expends the volley and still draws the beam. Only the "Missed!" wording is reproduced, not the original's "just missed" variant |
| Weapon damage | Phasers 0.20 to 2.00, photons 0.20 to 3.00 off the target's shields | Fire the same weapon repeatedly | Rolled per volley from the seeded RNG inside a symmetric band around the rescaled per-unit value: phasers 12 + 4 per unit at ±25%, photons 24 + 9 per unit at ±26.7%. Those spreads hold the manual's ratio of (max − min) to (max + min), 0.818 for phasers and 0.875 for photons, so average damage is unchanged and the spread alone neither lengthened nor shortened a war. Player and autopilot volleys share one `weaponDamage` roll. These figures are untouched by the later retune of war length, which doubled the hull complements instead of weakening the guns — see "Hull complements" |
| System damage | Scanner, transporter, and radio reach all depend on undamaged units, so damage has to be tracked per system | Take a ship below its shields and keep firing | Once shields are gone each further point is dealt to crew or subsystems, **weighted** so the crew (`CREW_DAMAGE_WEIGHT` slots per member against one per surviving subsystem unit) absorbs the bulk and the subsystems — weapons included — survive the fight. This corrects an earlier model that gave the whole complement a *single* slot against one per subsystem *type*: 200 crew were outranked eight-to-one, the ~28 subsystem units were stripped hundreds of points before the crew, and a knockout was therefore never capturable (measured 3% vacant). The volley now **stops the instant the crew reaches zero**; the hull is left `vacant` and can be taken over *unless* the damage left over in that volley reaches `OVERKILL_DESTROY_MARGIN` × the surviving subsystem units, in which case it tore the frame apart too and the hull is `destroyed`. Capture is thus a consequence of *how* the killing blow lands, not a fixed rate: a precise phaser finish captures ~55% of knockouts, a heavy photon finish ~35%, and ~84% of prizes keep at least one gun and their engines (mean 31% of the weapon complement). It redistributes damage between crew and subsystems without changing how much a hull absorbs in total, so war length is roughly held — an extended war is unchanged (23.1 vs 23.3 stardates over 250 seeds), while a classic attrition war runs ~14% shorter (18.5 vs 21.6 on the same harness) because hulls stay armed and resolve decisively instead of lingering as toothless hulks; classic capture share is 44%, extended 25% (lower because collisions, self-destructs, and doctrines add outright destructions). The two constants are the dials: raise the weight for better-armed prizes, raise the margin to capture more often |
| Tractor pull | Draws the target toward you and prevents movement | Use `5` on a target; then let an enemy beam catch you | Locks movement and pulls the target 5 units per tractor unit toward you. Autopilot beams call the same `tractorLock` helper and refuse targets past 35, so an enemy pull moves you exactly as far as yours moves it. A lock binds only while its caster is still active, so destroying the ship holding you frees you. The Xanadu starbase is the one exception: it is far too massive to tow, so a beam on it is refused outright and the autopilots never select it as a tractor target |
| Scanner | Maximum range 10 × undamaged scanner units | Use `6` on a target, then damage your scanner | Reach is recomputed from surviving units every turn; a scan past it is refused, and 0 units disables the command outright |
| Mapper | The map shows your surroundings "to the extent your ship's mapper is functioning" | Use `7`; damage the mapper | Fog of war and the local report both use 20 × surviving mapper units (the manual states no mapper formula); at 0 units the readout says "Mapper blacked out" |
| Transporter | Maximum range 10 × undamaged transporter units; reinforce a friendly ship or take over an empty one, and you may transfer your command | Use `8` on a friendly hull, then on a `vacant` one | Range enforced per surviving unit; crew reinforcement, capture of a vacant hull (which changes its allegiance), and the "Transfer command" option are all implemented |
| Radio | Maximum range 25 × active radio units; reports the location and condition of allied ships | Use `9` | Contacts within range answer with their alert level, distance, shields, crew, and status; at 0 units the command is refused |
| Hyperspace | A new position damages shields; a jump can go wrong | Use `-`, then confirm | Seeded 10% chance to burn up; otherwise relocates and loses 12% of shield capacity (min 5). The player does not aim it: the destination is drawn from the seeded RNG, and the command confirms first, since a jump that cannot be aimed cannot be walked back either. The manual describes only "a new position", so the landing point is a reconstruction rather than a recovered behavior. `hyperspaceAction` still honors explicit coordinates, which the tests use |
| Self-destruct | Nearby ships explode; a wider ring takes shrapnel | Use `=` near other ships | Blast radius 20 (starbase 40) destroys outright; a +15 shrapnel ring damages shields for `SHRAPNEL_DAMAGE` — 20 plus a seeded roll of 10 to 50. The blast needed no retune when the hull complements doubled, because it destroys rather than damages; the shrapnel did, being a flat number |
| Collisions | Ships that collide destroy one and cripple the other | Move onto a ship; autopilot navigation; tow a ship into another; hyperspace onto an occupied point | Seeded: one ship is destroyed and the other crippled — its shields burn off and it loses `CRIPPLE.fraction` (half) of its remaining crew and subsystem units, a share that cannot reduce a hull to nothing. A flat 120 damage previously killed *both* ships whenever the survivor was a scout, whose entire hull — shields, crew, and subsystems together — was smaller than that. Every overlapping pair at the arrival point now resolves rather than only the first, and collisions are checked after a tractor tow and a hyperspace arrival as well as after a move |
| Death in battle | "You're dead. The war will continue without you." | Lose your command ship | Command shifts to the strongest remaining Federation ship **that can move**; the war continues. Ranking on raw strength alone handed command to Xanadu, whose 320 shields and 320 crew out-mass every ship afloat but which has no engines — so losing the flagship parked the player on an immobile starbase for the rest of the war. Xanadu is still the successor when nothing else can maneuver, and resigning now calls the same function instead of keeping a second copy of the rule |
| Resignation | "Resign command and let the autopilot continue the war" | Press `Esc`, then press it again | Command shifts and the Federation runs on autopilot; you watch the war resolve (spectator mode). Resigning is final for that war: a second `Esc` is refused and input is ignored while the spectator loop runs. An open prompt owns the keyboard, so `Esc` dismisses the dialog instead of resigning |
| Visual map | Mapper shows your surroundings; ships identified by initial | Watch the map | Fog of war by mapper range; initials in circles; dashed phaser/photon/engine rings; red outline marks enemies that can reach you; `Backspace` reveals the whole zone |
| Condition | Alert level shown on the command panel: red, yellow, green | Watch the panel; damage a starbase and a scout by the same fraction | RED below 25% and YELLOW below 55% of the ship's own shield capacity, so every class reads alike at equal damage. This replaces fixed 25/55 point thresholds, which read a starbase as GREEN at 30% and a scout as distressed at 55%, and renames the old "DISTRESS" label |
| Stardate | The command panel displays the stardate | Watch the map header; press `R` | Shown as "Stardate N" in the map header and in the roll call title, advancing one per completed round |
| Battle narrative | The bottom of the screen carries reports and a scrolling narrative, "which may be abbreviated if your radio is damaged" | Damage your radio, then watch the log | Each entry is truncated to the surviving fraction of radio units; your own ship's lines stay whole, since those reach you over the intercom rather than the radio, and the panel header states the degradation |
| Autopilot | "`" runs the ship for one turn; autopilots are ruthless but clumsy | Press backtick | Autopilot pursues/fires/navigates with seeded drift, so it can collide |
| Formation | Fleets concentrate fire; suicide missions and tight packs collide | Watch an alliance engage | Non-vendetta ships focus the enemy nearest their flagship |
| Vendetta | One enemy ship hunts Captain Jason until he resigns or dies | Watch the vendetta ship; then board it with the transporter | It breaks formation to target the command ship, and the vendetta ends on resignation, death, or capture. The marker can never turn a ship against its own alliance or against itself |
| Surrender | The autopilot may surrender if conditions collapse | Reduce a fleet to its last ships | A fleet down to 2 ships at <=15% of opposing strength capitulates |

## Extended war (opt-in divergence)

An extended war is chosen in the **New game** panel and recorded on the game
state, so a save resumes in the mode it started in. Everything in the table above
still holds; the additions below are deliberate departures from the original,
which gave the player one hull and left the rest of the Federation to the same
autopilot as its enemies.

A classic war never reads these fields. `extended` alone changes no ship's
behavior until an order is issued, and a classic war's autopilot decisions are
identical to the calibrated ones — a test plays the same seed both ways and
compares the resulting fleets.

| Addition | Original behavior | How to see it | What the remake does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standing orders | The player commanded one hull; the rest of the Federation ran on the same autopilot as the enemy | Click a Federation hull on the map, or press `F` | Six per Federation hull: `focus` (the original's fleet concentration, and the default), `hold`, `withdraw`, `escort`, `screen`, `intercept`. Orders cost no turn and persist until changed. |
| Order delivery | — | Order a hull that is beyond radio range | An order is acknowledged the same stardate when the command ship's radio reaches the hull, or when Xanadu — with working radio — can hear both ends. Otherwise it is queued and lands at the end of the next computer phase, one stardate late. |
| Ordered navigation | Autopilots navigated with seeded drift and overshoot, and often collided | Compare an ordered hull with an unordered one | Ordered ships move directly, without the seeded drift and overshoot that makes unordered autopilots clumsy. The clumsiness is left to fleet-default behavior so that issuing orders is worth something. |
| Stale orders | — | Let the ship an order names be destroyed | An order naming a destroyed ship is dropped and that hull falls back to fleet behavior rather than idling. |
| Your own hull | Backtick ran the same autopilot the enemy used | Order your own ship, then press backtick | The command ship obeys its order whenever the autopilot has the conn: backtick for one turn, or the rest of the war after resigning. |
| Fleet report | — | Press `F` | Lists every Federation hull with its standing orders and radio contact. |
| Command buttons | `S` and `Backspace` were keyboard-only, so the README's "buttons provide the same controls" was false | Click the command panel | `S` (shot distribution) and `Backspace` (war zone map) gained buttons, so every command is reachable without the keyboard. |
| Dockyard support | No way to recover damage, so a long war only ever ran down | End a stardate inside the green ring around Xanadu | A hull inside `DOCKING.range` (8) of an active friendly starbase — one still holding at least half its own shields — that is not tractor-held recovers 8% of its shield capacity, 4 crew, and one unit of its most-damaged subsystem per stardate, each capped at the ship's complement. It resolves in the computer phase, is narrated, and is drawn as a green ring on the map. |
| Refits | — | Select a docked Federation hull and choose a refit | One refit per hull per war, at the dockyard only: +1 photons, +1 phasers, +1 engines, or +1 scanner and +1 mapper (`REFITS`). Refits add system units rather than hull capacity, so nothing downstream needs a capacity override, and no system may exceed its template complement by more than `REFIT_OVER_TEMPLATE` (2). Extended-only. Measured: holding Xanadu stays hard — a whole-fleet screen holds 20 of 60 at the retuned target of stardate 30, against 11 of 60 with no orders at all. |
| Fleet in motion | Ships teleported between stardates | Watch any computer phase | Each hull glides from its previous position to its new one over about half a second and leaves a fading dashed trail, so the computer phase reads as movement. Purely presentational: positions are unchanged, and both the glide and the trail are suppressed under `prefers-reduced-motion`. |
| Battle report | The war ended with a proclamation and a roll call | End any war | Adds stardates elapsed, Federation losses, top gun, heaviest punishment taken, most collisions, and the player's own record to the concluded panel. Collisions are now counted per hull (`collisions`), which is the only new per-ship field. |
| Alliance doctrines | The original ran every autopilot on one behavior: pursue the fleet's shared target, fire, and never mind its own skin | Watch an alliance fight in an extended war | Each alliance fights its own way, and only on hulls the player has not ordered — an order always outranks doctrine. **Axis** swarms the nearest hull at 6 units, refits below 20% shields, gives ground only below 8%, and self-destructs only as a last stand — below **2%** shields (retuned 8% → 4% → 2%, each step measured: one detonation kept ending wars on a wiped cluster) when at least **five** enemies — and more enemies than friends — sit inside its blast; the vendetta captain is exempt and keeps hunting. In a Reimagined war the blast itself is also scaled to 0.6× (`REIMAGINED_SELF_DESTRUCT_SCALE`) — see "Reimagined balance". **Bloc** works the phaser edge at 18, backs off anything inside 5, never tractors, and concentrates on the weakest hull it can actually hit. **Cabal** concentrates with the fleet but spends a tractor beam whenever the tow would smash the target into another enemy, never onto one of its own. **Federation** keeps the original's fleet concentration and adds damage discipline: refit below 35%, withdraw below 12%. |
| Autopilot self-preservation | None — the original's autopilots never flushed shields, retreated, or hyperspaced, so their shields only ever fell | Let a fleet fight in an extended war | Every doctrine can flush engines for shield power (narrated with the binary's own "has flushed its engines for shield power.") and fall back on its base or fleet. The vendetta ship is exempt from both, since the manual has it "numbly navigate through devastating enemy fire" in pursuit of Captain Jason. |
| Doctrine balance | — | Simulate 250 whole wars per configuration | Tuned against a full-war simulation so no alliance dominates. Over 250 seeds a classic war went Bloc 70 / Federation 70 / Axis 63 / Cabal 45 at a mean 24.7 stardates; an extended war with no player orders went Axis 107 / Federation 59 / Bloc 49 / Cabal 31 at 28.3. The hull-complement doubling left the classic spread essentially where it was and moved the extended one within about one standard deviation for three of the four alliances — Axis led the extended war at 101 of 250 before the change and 107 after — so no doctrine was retuned for it. **Cabal is the one shift outside that noise, down from 42 to 31**, and one extended war in 250 failed to terminate inside 600 stardates where none had before. Ordering three cruisers to screen Xanadu lifts the Federation, and mass-intercepting the Axis flagship hands the war to Bloc — both intended consequences of the dockyard and of removing one alliance early; those two order-set figures were measured before the doubling and have not been re-measured. The harness is now committed (`scripts/sim-wars.mjs`, `npm run sim`), and the self-destruct retunes have been re-measured on it (250 seeds): at 4%/four-enemies — the state after the first retune — an extended war went **Axis 34% / Federation 25.2% / Bloc 22% / Cabal 17.2%** at a mean 34 stardates, with 0.88 Axis last stands per war, an 11-hull worst blast, and a 4+-hull blast in 71.6% of wars. The second retune (2% shields, five enemies) pulled Axis back into the pack — **Federation 32.8% / Axis 29.2% / Cabal 20.8% / Bloc 16.8%** at a mean 34.9 — with last stands at 0.50/war and 4+-hull blasts in 48%. Absolute war lengths run longer than the 28.3 cited above because several rule changes landed since it was measured; compare spreads within one harness, not across eras. |
| Captains and aces | The manual names every ship but only one person, Captain Jason | Start an extended war, then scan an enemy hull | Every hull is dealt a captain from `CAPTAIN_NAMES` on a **separate seeded stream** (`<seed>:captains`), so ship positions and the vendetta pick are byte-identical to how they were before captains existed. `ACE_KILLS` (2) credited kills make an ace. The names are this remake's own expression, not the original's. |
| The vendetta hunt | One enemy ship has a vendetta against Captain Jason, and the player is never told which | Start an extended war | The opening narrative names the hunting *captain* but not the hull. Scanning a ship records it in `game.scanned` and reports its captain, so identifying your hunter means getting inside scanner range of the enemy fleet — which is what makes the scanner worth its hardware. |
| Escalating vendetta | The vendetta ship breaks formation to attack Jason's ship | Let the hunter score kills | Every `VENDETTA.killsPerStep` (3) kills, its volleys against your command ship gain `VENDETTA.damagePerStep` (25%) damage, applied through the same `weaponDamage` roll everything else uses, so player and autopilot volleys stay symmetric. Always zero in a classic war; the doctrine simulation was unchanged by it, since three kills rarely happen before the war is decided. |
| Kill narrative | ` destroyed.` wording | Destroy a hull in an extended war | Adds the destroying captain's name and kill tally, an ace announcement at two kills, and a grudge announcement on every third vendetta kill. A classic war's narrative is untouched — `killLines` returns nothing there. |
| Round replay | — | Fight a round, then press **Replay round** | The computer phase's events and narrative are kept as `game.lastRound`, and `replayEffects` redraws every ship's beams, torpedoes, and explosions. The existing `playEffects` only ever drew the ones involving your own ship, so roughly twenty autopilot decisions arrived as one wall of text with nothing to look at. |
| Impact juice | — | Take a hit; fall to RED | Phaser impacts flash, the map shakes when a volley lands on your command ship, and a klaxon sounds on the *transition* into RED rather than continuously. The shake and the effect animations are suppressed under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Cosmetic only — no rule reads any of it. |
| Scenarios | The original had one objective: destroy your enemies before they destroy you | Pick a scenario in the New game panel of an extended war | Three, all extended-only — a classic war always fights to annihilation. `annihilation` is the original condition. `defend-xanadu` wins with Xanadu still standing at stardate 30 (`SCENARIOS`) and loses the moment the base is destroyed. `hunt-the-vendetta` wins by ending the hull that hunts you **after** a scan has identified its captain, and loses if that hull leaves the war unidentified. Standard annihilation outcomes are checked first, so wiping out an alliance still wins outright, and the objective is stored separately from `vendettaShipId` because boarding the hunter clears the vendetta. |
| Mission panel | — | Fight any extended war | The report panel carries the scenario title, brief, and live progress. The hunt names your hunter's captain but never their hull, and never a position your sensors did not earn — the objective must not leak fog of war the way the `0` computer report does. |
| Scenario measurement | — | Simulate 60 wars per order set | Hold Xanadu at the retuned target of 30 stardates: screening with the whole fleet held 20 of 60, and no orders at all 11 of 60 — close to the 2:1 ratio the objective was originally tuned to. The target moved from 20 because doubling Xanadu's shields had made it trivial: at 20 those same fleets held 30 and 26 of 60, so issuing orders barely mattered. The base still falls in most wars (50 of 60 unordered, 52 of 60 screening; when it falls, the median stardate is 14). The three-cruiser screen was not re-measured. Hunt the hunter was not re-measured either — it turns on the scan rather than on attrition, though a longer war does give more stardates in which to get inside scanner range: never identifying the hunter lost 37 of 40, and identifying it won 37 of 40. |

## Precision fire (opt-in divergence)

Precision fire is chosen in the **New game** panel and recorded as `precision`
on the game state, so a save resumes with the dials it started with. It is
available in any war, classic or extended, and touches only the player's phaser
volleys: enemy captains and your own autopilot always fire standard volleys.
A war with the flag off never reads these rules — the power dial and called
system are ignored on arrival and the surrender sweep does not run; a test
fires the same dialed action into an unflagged war and compares the games.

| Addition | Original behavior | How to see it | What the remake does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Power dial | A phaser volley always fired at full output | Fire phasers in a precision war | The prompt's slider (0–100, default 100, sticky for the war) scales the rolled volley by `power / 100`. The roll itself is untouched, so at 100% the band is exactly the calibrated one, and the dial's use is the finishing blow: a throttled volley's overkill cannot reach the `OVERKILL_DESTROY_MARGIN` that shatters a hull, so a measured finish leaves a boardable prize. |
| Called subsystem shots | Subsystem damage fell at random, and because crew hold four candidate slots each it was almost always a late-fight phenomenon | Call a system in the phaser prompt | A called volley deals `SURGICAL_DAMAGE_FACTOR` (0.4) of the rolled volley, spends every penetrating point on the called system, takes no crew, and checks fire once that system is dead, losing the leftover damage. Shields still absorb the volley first, so calling is a post-shields tool: strip shields with standard fire, then operate. Photons scatter and can never be called. The no-crew rule is load-bearing — with the 4x crew weight, any volley that still rolled crew damage would barely touch the called system. |
| Disabled surrender | A hull burnt out of engines and guns fought on as a hulk | Burn out a hull's engines, phasers, and photons in a precision war | At stardate end an active hull with crew left but none of those three systems strikes its colors: the crew takes to escape pods and the hull is left `vacant`, boardable through the unchanged transporter rule — a capture path that kills no one. Starbases are exempt (a base never had engines, so burnt-out guns leave it a fortress) and so is your command ship while you have the conn. It resolves after the dockyard, so a disabled hull that limped home is repaired rather than surrendered, and before the fleet capitulation check, so a hull that struck its colors no longer counts toward its alliance's strength. |

Measured over 400 seeded volleys: a 40%-power finishing blow against a hull
worn to four crew left a boardable prize 174 times and destroyed the hull zero
times — the rest simply did not finish the crew. Whole-war simulation of
precision wars has not been run: the dials are player-only, and the autopilot
wars those simulations measure never read them.

The balance numbers these rules use, along with the ones the table above cites,
now live together in `game/constants.js` (`SHIP_TEMPLATES`, `WEAPONS`,
`MISS_CHANCE`, `RANGES`, `SHRAPNEL_DAMAGE`, `CRIPPLE`, `ALERT_THRESHOLDS`,
`SURRENDER`, `AI_PURSUIT`, `FLEET_ORDER_TUNING`,
`DOCKING`, `STALEMATE_ROUNDS`, `PERSONALITIES`, `CAPTAIN_NAMES`, `ACE_KILLS`,
`VENDETTA`, `SCENARIOS`, `SURGICAL_DAMAGE_FACTOR`) instead of scattered through
the modules, so retuning a row here means editing one file.

## Reimagined balance (play-test pass, opened 2026-09-19)

Reimagined is not calibrated against the DOS binary — there is nothing to
calibrate against — so its balance lives here instead, measured on the
committed harness (`npm run sim`; 250 seeds, the player's hull on the same
autopilot, no orders) and recorded per change. Nothing in this section touches
a classic or extended war: every Reimagined lever is gated on the flag, and the
parity tests hold.

Open findings for the rest of the balance pass:

- **Bloc surged to the lead at 38%** once carriers field drones (was 28.4% at
  round 19, behind the Federation's 31.6%). Its artillery-line archetype runs
  the hardest-hitting doctrine power profile (weapons 9 → 1.5× guns), and that
  bias now rides its drone screen too — three extra glass cannons that skip
  doctrine's retreat and just close and fire. Federation (balanced 1.0× guns)
  and Cabal (weakest 0.83× guns) get less out of the same bay. Watch item: if
  Bloc keeps dominating in play, the drone's reactor/phaser dials — or giving
  every alliance's drones the *flat* default profile instead of the doctrine
  one — are the levers, and both are measured, not guessed.
- **Cabal is still last** at 8.4% (9.2% before drones, 2% at baseline). The
  power-profile retune more than doubled its win rate; what remains is doctrine
  territory — the tractor-ram payoff on a wide field where collision tows need
  setup — plus the weakest drone guns of any alliance. Watch item: if Cabal
  keeps lagging in play, a Cabal doctrine pass can be its own chunk.
- **Timeouts rose to 5%** of Reimagined wars (4% before drones, 2% at baseline)
  and hopeless draws sit at 8%: more hulls on the field stretch endgames, and
  an orphaned wing that can still move keeps a war from stranding. If the long
  tail feels tedious in play, tighten `STALEMATE_ROUNDS` or revisit the regen
  rate — measured, not guessed.
